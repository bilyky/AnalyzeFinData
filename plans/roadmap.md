45. **"Riding Black Swans" &mdash; Antifragile Positive-Tail Capture (R&D #45, BACKLOG):** Nassim Taleb's positive-black-swan thesis applied to how AETHER *rides its winners*, not how it allocates them. You cannot forecast the rare, high-impact event &mdash; you can only engineer the **shape** of the payoff: hard-capped, known downside on every bet (ATR / defined-risk) plus **un-clipped upside** on the few that run, so one fat-tail outlier pays for a long series of small, cheap losses (convexity / positive skew). This is the **behavioural / payoff-shape layer that sits on top of #40's Barbell *allocation***: where **#40 (Shield/Spear)** sets *how much* risk goes into the asymmetric sleeve, this sets *what shape* the Spear's return distribution takes and *how the desk holds a runner*. It deliberately **binds, and does not restate,** machinery already on the roadmap &mdash; the Jul-25 **Intelligent-Failure Budget** (the small "fail often but cheaply" experimental slice), **#31 pyramiding** (SHIPPED anti-martingale scale-in into stop-locked winners), the **#10 Profit-Lock ratchet** + Jul-11 **Flower-Protection** + Aug-1 **fractal trailing stop** (the let-it-run exits), the **#28 CTSH / Jul-25(6a) sold-too-early** anti-pattern already *measured* by `decision_eval`'s missed-upside metric, and the **Rule C credit-spread** defined-risk overlay (#47, SHIPPED) plus the &ge;50%-cash mandate (the negative-swan shield). The distinctly **new** content this item owns (all backtest-first per the Jul-18 optimizer discipline, each independently toggleable):
    *   *(a) Convexity / expectancy scorecard &mdash; the missing yardstick:* In a convex book **win-rate is a lie** &mdash; you *expect* to lose most bets, so judging the Spear by hit-rate is exactly backwards and slowly kills the strategy. Add a `decision_eval`-style report of the Spear sleeve's payoff distribution &mdash; **expectancy, right-skew / tail-ratio, and whether E[return] is driven by a few outliers** &mdash; and grade the sleeve by expectancy + positive-tail-capture, never by win-rate. This metric does not exist today.
    *   *(b) Positive-tail exposure sizing:* You must have *enough* small, diversified convex bets on to actually be present when a positive tail fires &mdash; a rule for the *breadth* of the Spear (many cheap asymmetric bets, not 1&ndash;2 concentrated ones), distinct from #40's *how-much* sizing. Probability-of-capture, not conviction, drives the count.
    *   *(c) Ride-don't-clip exit (the direct antidote to #28):* For a Spear-sleeve name, **no fixed profit target &mdash; only a volatility-scaled trailing stop** (bind #10 + Jul-11 + the Aug-1 fractal trail), so upside is never clipped at a round R:R while downside stays hard-stopped. Selling a +63% winner that runs to +120% must score as a **process failure** (the missed-upside metric already flags it), not a booked win.
    *   *(d) Convex-bet budget & recycling:* Extend the Jul-25 Intelligent-Failure Budget into an explicit convex-bet book &mdash; many small **defined-risk** asymmetric entries (long optionality, RBR #19 higher-low re-entries, new-pattern experiments) sized "as small as possible," where losers expire cheap and a single winner pays for the book; tracked separately by **book-level expectancy**, expected to lose often.
    *   *(e) Survival-first (be in the game to collect the tail):* The negative black swan is the ruin risk &mdash; the Shield + a **defined-risk options** overlay (Rule C; explicitly **not** leveraged/inverse ETFs, per the Aug-8 mandate) must cap catastrophic downside so the desk stays solvent long enough for the positive tails to arrive. Aligns with the Rule of Loss Minimization and the &ge;50%-cash correction cushion.
    A future design doc `plans/riding-black-swans.md` would detail the scorecard math and the Spear exit/scale mechanics. Complements #40 (kept a separate, independently-trackable item, not folded in). **Status (2026-09-23): DOC-ONLY, UNBUILT** &mdash; concept intake; no convexity scorecard or Spear-ride execution layer exists on `main`, and nothing is wired until each thread clears its own backtest gate.
